2. Embedded baking-science rules

This small rule base maps common visible symptoms to possible causes. A symptom can have several causes, so test one change at a time.

  • Burnt / too dark: oven may be too hot, baking time too long, or top heat too strong.
  • Pale: temperature or time may be too low; sugar/formula and product type also affect browning.
  • Collapse: structure may not have set; possible causes include excess liquid/leavening, poor mixing, or opening the oven early.
  • Dense: insufficient aeration/leavening, excess liquid, incorrect ingredient ratios, or low oven temperature.
  • Dry / crumbly: overbaking, excess flour, or formula imbalance.
  • Excessive cookie spread: warm dough, soft/melted fat, or too little structure.
  • Cracks / peaked top: oven may be too hot, batter may be too stiff, or pan/formula may be unsuitable.
  • Gummy / underbaked: centre may need more baking; high heat can brown the outside before the centre sets.
These are troubleshooting hypotheses, not a guaranteed diagnosis. Exact causes depend on the recipe and the product.

3. Dataset and reference resources

These are external resources to download or inspect. Always check the licence, citation requirements, labels, and suitability before using a dataset in your project.

  • Bread defect images (Roboflow): 815 images; labels include crack, burnt, side_collapse, target_height and underbaked; listed as CC BY 4.0. Bread-focused, not a general cake/brownie dataset. Open dataset
  • AI bread quality dataset (USDA Ag Data Commons): bread colour/porosity images, metadata, porosity data and code; public-domain dedication CC0. Open dataset record
  • W²SB-3 bread crumb/crust dataset: bread images with physical measurements such as size, colour and hardness. Open Mendeley Data
  • Baking science / cake faults: common cake faults and possible causes. Read fault guide
  • Professional Baking textbook: oven conditions, cake structure and baking guidance. Read textbook PDF
  • Bakery troubleshooting guide: ingredient scaling, mixing, temperature and time. Read guide
To make this a real image AI: download a labelled dataset, prepare train/validation/test folders, train a CNN or transfer-learning model in Python, and connect its prediction API to this page. A dataset link alone does not train a model.
